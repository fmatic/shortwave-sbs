Listening guide by band

120 meter shortwave band

2300–2495 kHz · Night

Tropical-band listening; lower frequencies are strongly affected by daytime absorption. Look for regional broadcasts after sunset.

90 meter shortwave band

3200–3400 kHz · Night

Often useful for tropical and regional broadcasts. Quiet receiving locations help overcome atmospheric noise.

75 meter shortwave band

3900–4000 kHz · Evening and night

Try regional and international signals after dark; interference and regional allocations vary.

60 meter shortwave band

4750–5060 kHz · Evening and night

A useful tropical-band DX range, particularly around sunrise and sunset.

49 meter shortwave band

5900–6200 kHz · Evening and night

A popular international broadcast range. Darkness along the path often improves long-distance reception.

41 meter shortwave band

7200–7600 kHz · Evening and night

Often supports international reception during the evening and overnight. Allocations vary by region.

31 meter shortwave band

9400–9900 kHz · Day and evening

A versatile range for medium- and long-distance broadcasts, depending on season and solar activity.

25 meter shortwave band

11600–12100 kHz · Day and evening

Frequently useful for international paths during daylight and transitional hours.

22 meter shortwave band

13570–13870 kHz · Day

Try longer paths during daylight when ionospheric conditions support these frequencies.

19 meter shortwave band

15100–15800 kHz · Day

A classic daytime DX range, often useful for intercontinental reception.

16 meter shortwave band

How HF propagation affects shortwave reception

Shortwave signals can travel far beyond the horizon through ionospheric propagation. Lower frequencies often benefit from reduced absorption at night, while higher frequencies generally require sufficient ionization along the path. Solar activity, geomagnetic disturbances, season and the location of both transmitter and receiver all influence reception.

Greyline conditions near sunrise and sunset can sometimes favor particular paths. These effects are not guaranteed: local noise, antennas, transmitter power and interference also matter.

Find shortwave stations on air now

The shortwave.sbs live monitor combines EiBi, HFCC, AOKI and optional ITU HFBC schedule data. Select a band, enable the On air now filter and search by frequency, station or language. Use the DX Assistant and NOAA space weather information to explore potential listening opportunities.

All schedule times are in UTC. A scheduled broadcast is a listening candidate, not confirmation that a signal is being received at your location.
